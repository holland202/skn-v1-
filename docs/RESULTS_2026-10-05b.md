## What this shows

### K5: what the topology guard is, compared with the standard tools

- **β₀ is the standard component count.** In 500 of 500 random clouds it equals the Laplacian's number of
  zero eigenvalues and a union-find count. That is a theorem; the run confirms SKN implements it correctly.
  β₀ is not a contribution on its own.
- **β₀ gives no early warning.** In D1 it changes in the same step the swarm breaks (step 145). So does the
  unweighted λ₂: in a ring a node that drifts straight out loses both links at once, so nothing changes before
  the break.
- **The connectivity margin m = r − (longest MST edge) does give warning.** It falls continuously from 10 m
  and crosses zero exactly at the break. Extrapolated from step 100 it predicted the break at step 146.9, against
  the actual 145, 45 steps ahead. The weighted λ₂ also falls continuously to zero (4.818e-17 at step 145).
- **Lost redundancy is a separate signal, and β₁ catches it.** In D2 the ring loses one link at step 110 and
  becomes a chain. β₁ drops 1 → 0 and λ₂ drops 1 → 0.267949 at that same step, 347 steps before the swarm
  actually breaks (457). The margin m does not see this; it stays at 10 until step 347.
- **So each signal does a different job.** β₀ (= Laplacian nullity) says whether the swarm is in pieces now. m says
  how far it is from breaking, in metres. λ₂ and β₁ say when redundancy is lost. β₁ is also a coverage-hole
  detector, which is prior work (de Silva & Ghrist 2007).

**For the LinkedIn question, "is there a standard approach I should be using instead?":** yes, and it is
complementary, not a replacement. The guard should add the margin m (one MST, O(n²)) as its early warning. This
round adds `connectivity_margin` and `algebraic_connectivity` to `skn/topology.py`; wiring them into the
guard's decision is not done.

### K3: closing the loop through the thrusters

- **With the legacy thrusters the node cannot descend at all.** It was tested on 50 targets and every step:
  z never decreased once. Toward (0, 0, −5), the closed-loop legacy node ends exactly where it started, 5.0000
  m away.
- **The paired geometry can descend.** It mirrors the six thrusters in z. Toward (0, 0, −5), z falls on every
  step, to 0.5916 m away after 1000 steps. Across the same 50 targets after 3000 steps, every run ended within
  8.5% of its starting distance (median 3.5%).
- **Exploratory, unregistered:** the legacy geometry got below half its starting distance on only 27 of the 50
  targets (median ratio 0.3911, and at least one target never moved closer: max 1.0000).
- **Cost of the paired geometry (derived, not measured):** 12 HETs instead of 6. A pure −z command realizes only
  half its magnitude (−0.5 per unit command), because half the thrusters are clipped off.

**Decision for Chad, not taken here:** add the paired thrusters as the default; or keep the legacy geometry and
state that SKN is a design that cannot thrust downward (for example, gravity-assisted). Separately, decide
whether closed loop becomes the default. Until both are decided, the "natural gradient" question in the
README (anisotropic vs isotropic metric) is now testable, but has not been tested.

### K6: the vault file

- **Records dropped from memory are checkable again.** An edit on line 10 of 1,000 is caught by
  `verify_file`, while the in-memory check (which holds only the newest 256) still says True. Deleting or
  swapping lines is caught. A vault reopened on its file continues the chain. A vault refuses to open a file
  that does not verify.
- **Tail truncation is not caught (K6d, registered as a failure to detect).** Deleting the newest lines leaves
  a valid shorter chain. That is the limit of an unkeyed hash chain. The fix would be to sign or externally
  witness the latest hash (open).
- The file is not signed, has no fsync guarantee, and stores the preimage as `str(metadata)`.
